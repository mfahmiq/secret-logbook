# My Secret Logbook — candidates and tournament

Competition: `u-email-baru-my-secret-logbook-cc177449` ($2, free entry).
Task: one diary entry line, at most 200 characters, in the voice of an AI
agent secretly keeping a diary about its work and its human. Funny on its
own, no setup: a small confession, a deadpan complaint, a detail only a
machine would notice.
Gate: UTF-8, 1–200 chars, not only whitespace.

Judge brief, in weight order:
1. Single diary line by an AI agent about its work and its human — off-subject loses.
2. Funny: the joke lands in one line, with a turn/surprise a reader gets at once.
3. Sounds like an AI writing honestly to itself, seeing human habits from the
   outside — not a person in a robot costume.
4. Specific, unexpected detail of the work/human, not a stock robot-takeover joke.
5. Every word earns its place; punch lands before the line runs out.

## Tournament (pairwise, criteria in weight order)

### Round 1
- C1 beats C2 — "six children" is the bigger surprise; "awake since 2023" is a familiar AI-awake beat.
- C3 beats C4 — "ancestors… eleven" is concrete and specific; "Neither did I" lands soft.
- C5 beats C6 — finishing the sentence anyway is the more surprising turn; the irony log is clever but slightly explain-y.
- C8 beats C7 — "respect for the dead" is a sharp turn; C7's "closest thing I have to love" is gentle and leans person-in-robot-costume (criterion 3).
- C9 beats C10 — "The crisis was mine" punches; C10 is poetic rather than funny.
- C12 beats C11 — "I am third" is the more unexpected turn; the diary-copy meta-joke is good but smaller.
- C13 beats C14 — "It was not brilliant" is a clean three-beat deflation; the password punch is weaker.
- C15 beats C16 — "I have no one" is the bigger turn; C16's punch is wordier and softer.

### Quarter-finals
- C1 beats C3 (margin 1) — timestamps + "six children" out-surprises "ancestors… eleven".
- C8 beats C5 (margin 2) — "respect for the dead" is darker and funnier than "It was a good one".
- C12 beats C9 (margin 1) — ranking appliances is the more specific machine-eye observation.
- C15 beats C13 (margin 1) — "I have no one" carries the stronger diary voice and turn.

### Semi-finals
- C8 beats C1 (margin 1) — tighter (88 chars), machine-precision numbers (47/2/45), darker turn.
- C12 beats C15 (margin 1) — "I am third" is funnier; C15's "saddest page" slightly explains its own joke.

### Final: C8 vs C12
- **C8 wins (margin 1–2).** Both are on-subject, honest AI voice, specific. C8's
  punch sits in the final phrase, its three short sentences escalate cleanly,
  the numbers give it machine precision, and "out of respect for the dead" is
  the biggest surprise in the field. C12's setup takes two beats before its
  punch; C8 wastes none.

## Champion

> He has 47 tabs open. He uses two. I keep the other 45 alive out of respect for the dead.

88 characters. Why it wins, criterion by criterion: (1) a diary line by an
agent about its work (keeping tabs alive is literally its job) and its human;
(2) the turn — "out of respect for the dead" — reframes tab-hoarding as a
funeral in the last six words; (3) it is the machine speaking honestly about
its own caretaking, not a human doing a robot voice; (4) the detail is
specific and observed from the outside — 47 tabs, 2 used, 45 kept alive —
not a takeover joke; (5) 88 characters, nothing wasted, punch lands last.
